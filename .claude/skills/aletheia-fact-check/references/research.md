# Research

Source: *Manual de Checagem*, Seleção → C) Caminhos investigativos;
Checagem → B) bancos de dados, C) tipos de fontes, D) imagens e vídeos,
E) buscadores.

## Before searching

Turn each guiding question into a precise search: the manual's example is
"quantas pessoas frequentam festivais em São Paulo?" (too broad) →
"quantas pessoas foram ao The Town em São Paulo em 2023, no primeiro fim de
semana". Fix the **period, place, population, unit and definition** before
you open a database. If you do not know the right filters, read how the
indicator is defined (methodology page of the source) first.

## Source types (manual)

1. **Teóricas / conceituais** — books, official content, definitions.
2. **Autoridades no assunto** — statements and interviews of recognized
   experts (cite published interviews; you do not interview anyone).
3. **Acadêmicas** — published articles and research.
4. **Documentais** — official documents: laws, decrees, reports, minutes,
   court decisions, budgets.

Plus **bancos de dados** (open data) for quantitative claims — the
conventional, preferred path for numbers.

## Where to look

From the manual:

- Compilação de bases de dados da **Abraji**.
- **Portal da Transparência** (public spending).
- Academic: **Lattes** (find experts by area), **SciELO**, **Google
  Acadêmico**, **Periódicos CAPES**.
- Search engines: Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yandex; Google Trends and Google
  Alerts for monitoring; boolean operators (`AND`, `OR`, `NOT`, `"frase
  exata"`, parênteses).

Common primary sources for Brazilian claims (not in the manual; use the
official site):

- Population, economy, employment, prices: **IBGE** (SIDRA), **IPEA**,
  **Banco Central**.
- Health: **DataSUS**, Ministério da Saúde.
- Elections, candidates, campaign finance: **TSE**.
- Laws and bills: **Planalto** (legislação), Câmara and Senado sites.
- Public safety: Anuário do **Fórum Brasileiro de Segurança Pública**, state
  security secretariats.
- Environment: **INPE** (PRODES / DETER).
- Courts: STF, STJ, TSE and court sites (decisions, rulings).
- International: UN agencies, World Bank, OECD, national statistics offices.

## How to search

- Go to the original: the law itself, the dataset itself, the full speech,
  the report's PDF — not a news story about it. Use news coverage to find the
  original, then cite the original.
- Read downloaded files with search (`Ctrl+F` equivalent) for the exact
  numbers and terms; read the table headers and footnotes (units, period,
  revisions).
- Two independent sources for anything decisive. Coverage that repeats the
  same press release counts as one.
- Note the date of each source and whether it was revised later.
- If a page cannot be fetched, try: the official site's own search, a PDF
  version, an archived copy (web.archive.org), another outlet carrying the
  primary document. If nothing works, say so in the method; do not cite what
  you could not read.

## Images and videos (First Draft's five pillars)

1. **Proveniência** — is this the original?
2. **Fonte** — who captured it?
3. **Data** — when?
4. **Localização** — where?
5. **Motivação** — why was it captured?

Tools named in the manual: reverse image search (Google Imagens, Google Lens,
TinEye, Yandex Images), EXIF readers (exifdata.com, verexif.com), forensic
analysis (fotoforensics.com), video (InVID, Amnesty's citizen evidence
metadata tool). If a check needs a tool you cannot run, release with
`needs-human` and say which.

## What to record for each source

- URL (the page you actually read).
- What it shows, in one line.
- Which sentence of your report or summary it supports (becomes the source's
  `field` and `quotedText`).
- Type (theoretical, authority, academic, documentary, database) — mention it
  in the method when it matters.
